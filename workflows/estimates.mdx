The estimate is where a maybe becomes booked work. Build the quote on screen while the customer watches or waits, send a link by text or email, and they approve and pay the deposit in one sitting. Approval does the paperwork for you: the job appears on your calendar queue and a draft invoice is already waiting.

## Creating an estimate

Go to **Estimates** and click **New Estimate**. The builder walks through four steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Select a customer">
    Search by name, phone, or email. New customer? Add them right from the search panel without leaving the builder.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Select a vehicle">
    Pick a vehicle on file or add one with year, make, model, and color. ShopWrk suggests the pricing tier from the vehicle size.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add services">
    Browse your catalog by category. Prices fill in from the vehicle tier automatically. Mark anything Optional to let the customer decide at approval time.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and set the deposit">
    Check totals, apply a discount or tax override, and set the deposit amount. Then send by email, SMS, or both.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  The tier selector sits above the services panel. A lifted truck wearing sedan prices fixes itself in one click before the quote ever goes out.
</Tip>

## Tier-based pricing

Each service can carry a price per vehicle size. The customer-facing quote can present package tiers, typically Good / Better / Best, so buyers self-select instead of you guessing their budget:

| Vehicle size | Typical fit |
| --- | --- |
| Sedan | Cars and coupes |
| SUV | SUVs, crossovers, minivans |
| Truck | Full-size trucks and large vans |

Prefer one flat price? Turn tiers off and every line item uses a single number regardless of vehicle.

## Sending and tracking

From the estimate page, click **Send**. Email arrives branded with your logo; SMS lands as a short link. Both open the same token-authenticated page, so customers never need an account.

Every status change shows in your estimates list:

| Status | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Draft** | Still being built, not sent |
| **Sent** | With the customer, waiting |
| **Viewed** | They opened the link |
| **Approved** | Accepted, deposit handled, job created |
| **Declined** | Passed, reason attached if they left one |
| **Expired** | Past the valid-until date you set |

Set a **Valid until** date so stale quotes stop being approvable. Follow-up automations fire off the Sent and Viewed states without anyone remembering to nudge.

## What the customer sees

Your branding, the line items, tier options where enabled, and optional add-ons as checkboxes. A live total updates as they choose. When they are ready, Approve and pay happen on the same page: if the estimate carries a deposit requirement, card entry comes first and approval completes once the deposit clears. No deposit on the quote? Approval is one click.

If a document needs signing before approval, like a liability waiver for a wrap, the customer signs it inline before they can continue. Questions typed into the estimate page land in your [conversations inbox](/features/communications).

<Info>
  Deposits are the quiet close rate booster. A customer who has money down does not shop the quote around overnight. Most shops require 20 to 50 percent depending on ticket size and film cost.
</Info>

## After approval

Approval triggers the chain without a click from you:

1. The job is created with services, vehicle, and deposit status carried over. Scheduled date set? It lands on the calendar. Not scheduled yet? It waits in the Waiting queue until you drag it onto a day.
2. A draft invoice exists with the deposit already credited.
3. Your estimate-approved automation fires, if you turned one on.

You can still schedule straight from the estimate if you prefer to pick the bay and tech yourself; the conversion screen carries everything over either way.

<Note>
  An estimate converts once. After that it links to the job it created so history stays traceable.
</Note>
